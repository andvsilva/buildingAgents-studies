# Variational and Optimization Algorithms in Quantum Computing

---

Quantum computing has developed rapidly in recent decades as a new paradigm for information processing based on the fundamental principles of quantum mechanics. Unlike classical computers, which use bits that assume binary values ($0$ or $1$), quantum computers use **qubits**, which can exist in a **superposition of states**.

---

# 1. Introduction

Quantum computers exploit fundamental properties of quantum mechanics, such as:

* **Superposition**
* **Entanglement**
* **Quantum interference**

to perform computations in ways that differ significantly from classical computers.

However, current quantum devices belong to what is known as the:

**NISQ Era — Noisy Intermediate-Scale Quantum**

This means that current quantum hardware:

* contains **a limited number of qubits**
* suffers from **noise and operational errors**
* cannot support **very deep quantum circuits**

For this reason, a class of algorithms known as:

## Variational Quantum Algorithms (VQAs)

has emerged.

These algorithms combine:

* **quantum computation**
* **classical optimization**

The general procedure is:

1. Prepare a **parameterized quantum state** using a quantum circuit.
2. Measure a **cost function**.
3. Use a **classical optimizer** to update the parameters.
4. Repeat the process until a minimum is found.

This iterative procedure is called the:

**Quantum–Classical Hybrid Loop**

These algorithms are currently considered the **most promising approach for NISQ hardware**.

Among the most important ones are:

* **VQE — Variational Quantum Eigensolver**
* **QAOA — Quantum Approximate Optimization Algorithm**
* Extensions and new methods inspired by **metaheuristics** or **quantum control techniques**.

In the following sections, we examine these algorithms in more detail.

---

# 2. Variational Quantum Eigensolver (VQE)

## Objective

The VQE algorithm was proposed to solve the problem of finding the **minimum eigenvalue of a Hamiltonian**

$$
H |\psi\rangle = E |\psi\rangle
$$

This problem appears in several areas, including:

* quantum chemistry
* condensed matter physics
* nuclear physics
* many-body physics

The goal is to determine the **ground-state energy** of a system.

---

## Variational Principle

The algorithm relies on the **variational principle of quantum mechanics**

$$
E_0 \le \langle \psi(\theta) | H | \psi(\theta) \rangle
$$

where

* $E_0$ = ground-state energy
* $|\psi(\theta)\rangle$ = parameterized quantum state

Thus, by minimizing

$$
E(\theta) = \langle \psi(\theta) | H | \psi(\theta) \rangle
$$

we approximate the ground state.

---

# Structure of the VQE Algorithm

The algorithm has three main components:

1. **Hamiltonian**
2. **Variational Ansatz**
3. **Classical optimizer**

---

# Steps of the VQE Algorithm

## 1 — Hamiltonian Mapping

First, the physical Hamiltonian must be mapped to qubits.

For example (in quantum chemistry):

$$
H = \sum_i c_i P_i
$$

where

$$
P_i \in {I, X, Y, Z}^{\otimes n}
$$

Each term corresponds to **tensor products of Pauli operators**.

---

## 2 — Ansatz Construction

The quantum state is prepared using a parameterized circuit

$$
|\psi(\theta)\rangle = U(\theta)|0\rangle
$$

The unitary operator is constructed using **parameterized quantum gates**.

A typical example:

```
|0> ──RY(θ1)──●────────RY(θ3)──
              │
|0> ──RY(θ2)──X────────RY(θ4)──
```

Common gates used include:

* **RY**
* **RX**
* **RZ**
* **CNOT**

These gates generate:

* rotations in Bloch space
* entanglement between qubits

---

## 3 — Energy Measurement

The energy is calculated as

$$
E(\theta) = \sum_i c_i \langle P_i \rangle
$$

Each term is measured separately.

---

## 4 — Classical Optimization

A classical optimization algorithm adjusts the parameters.

Examples include:

* Gradient Descent
* COBYLA
* SPSA
* Nelder–Mead

The goal is to compute

$$
\theta^* = \arg \min E(\theta)
$$

---

## 5 — Convergence

When the minimum is reached,

$$
|\psi(\theta^*)\rangle \approx |\psi_0\rangle
$$

---

# Role of Quantum Gates in VQE

Quantum gates determine several key aspects of the algorithm.

### 1 — Expressibility of the Ansatz

More gates → greater ability to represent complex quantum states.

### 2 — Circuit Depth

Deep circuits:

* increase expressibility
* introduce more noise

### 3 — Entanglement

Entanglement is generated by gates such as:

* CNOT
* CZ

This is essential for representing **correlated quantum systems**.

---

# Advantages of VQE

* suitable for NISQ hardware
* relatively shallow circuits
* important applications in quantum chemistry

---

# Challenges of VQE

### Barren Plateaus

Regions where gradients become extremely small.

### Experimental Noise

Includes readout errors and decoherence.

### Ansatz Design

There is **no universal ansatz construction method**.

---

# 3. Quantum Approximate Optimization Algorithm (QAOA)

## Objective

QAOA is designed to solve **combinatorial optimization problems**, such as:

* Max-Cut
* SAT
* Graph Partitioning

---

## Core Idea

The optimization problem is encoded into a **cost Hamiltonian**

$$
H_C
$$

The goal is to minimize

$$
\langle H_C \rangle
$$

---

# Structure of QAOA

The circuit is built by alternating two operators:

### Cost Operator

$$
U_C(\gamma) = e^{-i\gamma H_C}
$$

### Mixer Operator

$$
U_B(\beta) = e^{-i\beta H_B}
$$

---

# QAOA Circuit

The final quantum state is

$$
|\psi(\gamma,\beta)\rangle =
\prod_{k=1}^{p}
U_B(\beta_k)U_C(\gamma_k)|+\rangle^{\otimes n}
$$

---

# Implementation with Quantum Gates

If

$$
H_C = Z_i Z_j
$$

then

$$
e^{-i\gamma Z_i Z_j}
$$

can be implemented using:

```
CNOT
RZ
CNOT
```

---

# QAOA Algorithm Steps

1. Prepare the uniform superposition state

$$
|+\rangle^{\otimes n}
$$

2. Apply the cost operator
3. Apply the mixer operator
4. Measure the cost function
5. Optimize the parameters

---

# Benefits of QAOA

* applicable to NP-hard problems
* relatively shallow circuits
* hybrid quantum-classical framework

---

# Challenges

* difficult parameter optimization
* scalability issues
* noise sensitivity

---

# 4. Quantum Whale Optimization Algorithm (QWOA)

QWOA is inspired by the classical algorithm:

**Whale Optimization Algorithm (WOA)**

which simulates the hunting behavior of whales.

---

## Classical Algorithm Idea

Whales use a hunting strategy called:

**bubble-net feeding**

which captures prey using a spiral-shaped movement.

---

# Quantum Adaptation

QWOA uses:

* quantum superposition
* quantum interference

to explore the search space.

---

# Quantum Representation

A solution is represented by

$$
|x\rangle
$$

and a population is represented as

$$
|\psi\rangle = \sum_x \alpha_x |x\rangle
$$

---

# Quantum Operators

Operators equivalent to WOA steps include:

### Global Search

Diffusion operator.

### Local Exploration

Controlled rotations.

### Position Update

Unitary transformations.

---

# Gates Used

Typical gates include:

* Hadamard
* Rotation gates
* Controlled gates

These modify the **probability amplitudes of candidate solutions**.

---

# Algorithm Flow

1. Initialize the system in superposition
2. Evaluate the objective function
3. Update probability amplitudes
4. Use constructive interference
5. Collapse to the optimal solution

---

# Benefits

* parallel exploration of the search space
* potential quantum speedup

---

# Challenges

* difficult physical implementation
* requires complex circuits

---

# 5. BF-DCQO (Bias-Field Digitized Counterdiabatic Quantum Optimization)

This algorithm is based on:

* **Adiabatic Quantum Computing**
* **Counterdiabatic quantum control**

---

# Adiabatic Quantum Computing

A quantum system evolves from

$$
H_0
$$

to

$$
H_P
$$

slowly over time.

---

## Time-dependent Hamiltonian

$$
H(t) = (1 - s(t))H_0 + s(t)H_P
$$

---

# Problem

The evolution must be **very slow** to remain in the ground state.

---

# Counterdiabatic Control

An additional term is introduced:

$$
H_{CD}
$$

which suppresses unwanted transitions to excited states.

---

# Total Hamiltonian

$$
H(t) = H_0 + s(t)H_P + H_{CD}
$$

---

# Digitized Counterdiabatic Evolution

On gate-based hardware, the evolution is discretized as

$$
U(t)=e^{-iH_1\Delta t}e^{-iH_2\Delta t}
$$

---

# Role of the Bias Field

The bias field helps to:

* avoid local minima
* accelerate convergence

---

# Gates Used

The evolution is decomposed into:

* rotation gates (RZ)
* rotation gates (RX)
* interaction gates (ZZ)

---

# Algorithm Flow

1. Prepare the initial state
2. Apply a sequence of digitized evolution steps
3. Apply counterdiabatic terms
4. Measure the energy

---

# Benefits

* accelerates adiabatic algorithms
* suppresses excited-state transitions
* improves convergence

---

# Challenges

* constructing the counterdiabatic term
* circuit depth
* noise sensitivity

---

# 6. Comparison Between Algorithms

| Algorithm | Application                | Type            |
| --------- | -------------------------- | --------------- |
| VQE       | quantum chemistry          | variational     |
| QAOA      | combinatorial optimization | variational     |
| QWOA      | quantum metaheuristic      | nature-inspired |
| BF-DCQO   | adiabatic optimization     | quantum control |

# 7. Conclusion

Variational algorithms represent one of the **most promising approaches for current quantum computing hardware**.

They allow researchers to:

* work effectively with **NISQ devices**
* solve problems in **physics and optimization**
* explore **hybrid quantum–classical paradigms**

However, important challenges remain:

* quantum noise
* barren plateaus
* scalability
* development of efficient ansätze.
